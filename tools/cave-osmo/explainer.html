Osmo Mobile 7 Protocol

How to drive the OM7 phone gimbal from your own software: the link, the message layout, what it reports and the commands that move it. Everything on this page was worked out from captures of DJI Mimo and tested on the gimbal; things that were tried and do nothing are listed as such.

Bonding trap

macOS and iOS bond the gimbal as a HID device on first connect and reconnect it at once; it then stops advertising, and a scan never finds it again. Also ask the OS for already-connected peripherals with service fff0 (retrieveConnectedPeripherals(withServices:) on Apple platforms).

Only one central at a time: a phone running Mimo, or a Mac that grabbed it, blocks everyone else.

3 · Starting a session

Answer the handshake, then it reports

Right after connecting, the gimbal asks to be acknowledged. Answer with the same sequence number and it starts sending its attitude once a second. Ignore it and it stays quiet.

Use it like this

  • Aim by the quaternion. Each speed command turns exactly one Euler angle of it (yaw, pitch, roll).
  • Guard limits by the joints. With the clamp on its side, one commanded axis moves several joints.
  • Once a second is the rate. Polling (04/02) gets no answer.

Thumb stick (27→02, 04/57)

Payload: x, y as int16, ±660 at full push. With an app connected the gimbal forwards the stick to the app instead of moving itself.

Send every ~100 ms while turning; it stops ~0.2 s after the last one. Good for a joystick. Timed turns come up about 2° short. Speed commands can flip the clamp: past a roll joint of about 63 or 221 (portrait) it swings over violently, at any speed. For going to a direction, use target mode.

6 · Moving it to an angle

Target mode: DJI's own positioning

How Mimo runs a panorama. You send a list of attitude targets relative to home (not motor angles; the gimbal works out its motors itself) and step through them. It never flips, takes each move in the time you give it, and says when it has arrived. Bench accuracy: every stop within 0.2–3°.

Rules that keep it clean

  1. 15 targets per list at most. 16 or more are accepted and never stepped through.
  2. One list at a time. To send the next, leave, wait for status 00, enter again, wait ~2.5 s, upload.
  3. End every list at home. Entering sends the gimbal home, so a list that ends there makes the next entry a non-move.
  4. Don't go quiet for 9 s. Target mode ends by itself; to hold a target longer, send its "go" again (it is already there, nothing moves).
  5. Mind the yaw range: about −200° … +95°. +120 is clamped, +176 … 180 refused; ask for the same heading 360° lower.
  6. Roll: the + side reaches at least +110 (20° past level in portrait). The − side is capped by the gimbal near −77, and with a load hanging out sideways a quick move there overloads it.
  7. A stuck "moving" on the first target of a fresh list: send its go once more.

Where a target points (portrait, load along body +Y)

Targets are attitudes relative to home. A load in the clamp makes it sag back at home (about 16° for ~150 g); that sag tilts the whole yaw axis. Model it rather than command it away:

v = Ry(sag) · Rz(yaw) · (0, cos ra, sin ra) with ra = −90° − roll
inclination = asin(−vdown) azimuth = atan2(veast, vnorth)

Fits every bench point within 2°. Each direction has two solutions (the two roll sides); straight up is reachable by leaning the sag back out with roll.

8 · The hardware

Things the gimbal does to what it carries

  • Its clamp holds a strong magnet. A compass in the clamp is useless; distances and inclinations are fine. Take azimuth from the gimbal's own attitude, relative to where it woke up.
  • A load sags it at home and makes short-side moves risky (above).
